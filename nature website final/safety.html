Stay Safe in the Natural State

Essential tips for a safe and enjoyable outdoor experience.

🌲 Trail Safety

  • Stay on marked trails: Off-trail hiking can lead to getting lost or damaging local flora.
  • The "Buddy System": Never hike alone in unfamiliar territory.
  • Wildlife: Keep a safe distance from all wildlife and never feed them.

🎒 Packing Essentials

Before you head out, ensure your pack includes these safety basics:

  • Bear Spray:Keep it in a holster on your belt or pack strap, not buried inside your bag.
  • First Aid Kit:Include antihistamines, ibuprofen, and Tecnu (or similar soap) to wash off plant oils.
  • Communication:A whistle is much louder than a human voice if you become lost or injured.
  • Navigation:A physical map and compass are reliable backups for when GPS signals fail or batteries die.

Wildlife Awareness and Saftey

Most wild animals are naturally wary of humans and will avoid you if they hear you coming. The goal is to prevent surprises.

  • Make Noises:Talk, sing, or clap, especially when moving through dense brush or near running water.
  • Store Food Away Properly:Never leave food or scented items (like toothpaste) in your tent. Use bear-resistant containers or hang a "bear bag" at least 12 feet up and 6 feet out from a tree trunk.
  • Observe from a Distance:If you see an animal, give it plenty of space. Never get between a mother and her young. Remeber that these are wild animals not pets

Identifying Poisonous Plants

The "Leaves of Three, Let It Be" rule is a classic for a reason, but it doesn't cover everything. Here are the primary culprits to watch for:

  • Poison Ivy:Can grow as a vine or a shrub. It always has three leaflets, and the middle leaf usually has a longer stalk than the two side leaves.
  • Poison Oak:Similar to poison ivy but the leaves are lobed, resembling the leaves of an oak tree.
  • Poison SumacGrows in very wet soil (swamps/bogs). It has 7 to 13 leaflets arranged in pairs with a single leaf at the end.
