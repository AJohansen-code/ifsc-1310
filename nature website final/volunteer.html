Give Nature a Hand

Protect the Natural State: Volunteer with Arkansas Parks

Arkansas is home to some of the most stunning landscapes in the country, from the peaks of the Ozarks to the winding waters of the Delta. These treasures stay beautiful and accessible because of people like you. Whether you are a student, a retiree, or just someone who loves the outdoors, your time makes a direct impact on our environment and our community.

How You Can Help

There are volunteer opportunities to fit every skill set and schedule:

  • Trail Maintenance: Help design, build, and clear the paths that allow millions of visitors to explore our parks.
  • Park Beautification: Get your hands dirty planting native flowers, trees, and shrubs, or help with painting and facility upkeep.
  • Education & Programs: Share your passion by assisting with interpretive programs, teaching outdoor skills, or helping at visitor centers.
  • Adopt-a-Park: Groups or individuals can take "ownership" of a specific local park or trail, committing to regular clean-ups to keep it pristine.
  • Campground Hosting: For those who love the park lifestyle, hosts live on-site (often with provided utilities) to assist visitors and keep the campgrounds running smoothly.

Taking it to the Next Level: Become a Park Ranger

If you want to make conservation your life's work, consider becoming a Park Ranger. Rangers are the guardians of our resources, serving as educators, conservationists, and law enforcement officers.

  • The Path to the Badge: Most full-time ranger positions in Arkansas require a bachelor's degree in fields like Natural Resource Management, Earth Sciences, or Law Enforcement.
  • The Responsibility: Rangers manage wildlife protection, search and rescue operations, and public safety while ensuring every visitor leaves with a deeper appreciation for nature.
  • The Reward: It is more than a job—it's a career dedicated to protecting Arkansas's heritage for future generations.
