Hiking Arkansas Natural Trails

Exploring Arkansas: A Hiker's Guide to the Natural State

Arkansas offers everything from easy lakeside strolls to rugged mountain climbs. Whether you’re a first-timer or a seasoned trekker, staying safe and respecting the landscape ensures these trails remain beautiful for everyone.

Trail Recommendations by Experience Level

  • Beginner - Kingfisher Trail (Pinnacle Mountain): A paved, level path through the wetlands. Perfect for a relaxed walk under cypress trees.
  • Beginner - Lost Valley Trail (Buffalo River): Features a natural bridge and waterfalls with relatively flat terrain and clear signage.
  • Intermediate - West Summit Trail (Pinnacle Mountain): A steady climb that offers a workout with a high-reward 360° view of Central Arkansas.
  • Intermediate - Cedar Falls Trail (Petit Jean): A moderately strenuous hike leading to one of the most iconic 95-foot waterfalls in the state.
  • Experienced - East Summit Trail (Pinnacle Mountain): This is a rugged "scramble" over boulder fields. It requires balance and focus—no paved paths here!
  • Experienced - Big Goat Trail (Rattlesnake Ridge): Offers technical terrain and steep climbs for those looking for a more remote, rugged feel.

Safety Essentials: Before You Step Out

Arkansas weather and terrain can be unpredictable. Follow these Golden Rules for a smooth trip:

  • Hydration is Non-Negotiable: Pack at least 1 liter of water per person, per hour, especially during the humid summer months. Don't wait until you're thirsty to drink.
  • The "Tell a Friend" Rule: Always let someone know which trail you are hitting and when you expect to be back. Cell service can be spotty in deep hollows.
  • Tick & Critter Awareness: Ticks and chiggers are active most of the year. Wear light-colored clothing (to spot them easily), use repellent with DEET, and always do a full-body check after your hike.
  • Respect the Pack It In, Pack It Out Philosophy: Whatever you bring into the park—wrappers, bottles, or peels—must leave with you.
